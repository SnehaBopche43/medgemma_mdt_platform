**1. Treatment Strategy:**

Given the Stage IV nature of the colorectal cancer, the treatment strategy should focus on systemic therapy to control the disease and potentially achieve long-term remission. The specific approach will depend on the patient's overall health, performance status, comorbidities, and the results of the pathology report.

*   **Recommended systemic therapy approach:**
    *   **First-line systemic therapy:** The standard first-line treatment for metastatic colorectal cancer is typically a combination of chemotherapy and immunotherapy.
        *   **Chemotherapy:** A common regimen is FOLFOX (5-Fluorouracil, Leucovorin, Oxaliplatin) or FOLFIRI (5-Fluorouracil, Leucovorin, Irinotecan). The choice depends on the patient's tolerance and specific tumor characteristics.
        *   **Immunotherapy:** Pembrolizumab (Keytruda) is often used in combination with chemotherapy, especially if the tumor has high PD-L1 expression.
    *   **Alternative first-line regimens:** Other options may include combinations of chemotherapy and immunotherapy based on the latest guidelines and clinical trial data.

*   **Specific chemotherapy regimen if indicated:**
    *   Based on the pathology report, the choice of chemotherapy regimen (FOLFOX vs. FOLFIRI) will be determined.
    *   The dose of chemotherapy will be adjusted based on the patient's performance status and renal function.

*   **Hormonal therapy recommendations:**
    *   Hormonal therapy (e.g., LHRH agonists, CDK4/6 inhibitors) is generally considered for patients with hormone receptor-positive (HR+) colorectal cancer. The presence of ER and PR will determine the need for hormonal therapy.

*   **Targeted therapy considerations:**
    *   The presence of HER2 overexpression will determine the need for targeted therapy (e.g., Trastuzumab, Pertuzumab).
